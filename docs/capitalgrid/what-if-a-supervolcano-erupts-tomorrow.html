Frequently Asked Questions
Q1. What do the five timeline phases represent?
They model the first 24 hours after a Yellowstone supervolcano eruption: magma rise (0–60 min), Plinian eruption at hour 3 (~100,000 m³/s), pyroclastic fury at hour 6 (700 km/h, 1,000 °C), ash blanket at hour 12 (30 cm), and global winter at hour 24 (−10 °C drop).
Q2. How is the canvas visualization computed?
The canvas renders a cross-section of the magma chamber, eruption column, and ash blanket. The height, width, and opacity of each element are driven by the slider position and the underlying physical model (exponential magma pressure, column height, and ash accumulation).
Q3. What does the CSV export contain?
The export includes the current time stamp, phase label, magma pressure, eruption column height, pyroclastic speed, ash thickness, global temperature drop, and ejecta rate — all in SI-compatible units.
Q4. Can I share a specific scenario?
Yes. The "Copy Scenario Link" button copies the current URL with a ?t= query parameter representing the elapsed minute, so the exact state can be reloaded and shared.
