Frequently Asked Questions

Does this simulation match the video's $242,000 figure exactly?

The video's anchor series places the 15-year NVDA portfolio at $242,000 after $27,375 of cumulative deposits. This workstation models that same case with a ~45% annualized return and monthly $150 contributions. Adjusting the sliders changes the trajectory in real time, so you can see how fragile the $242k outcome is.

Why does the "Coffee Spend" series spike to $27,375?

That value represents the cumulative cost of a $5/day habit compounded over 15 years — $1,825 per year × 15 = $27,375. The intermediate payload anchors (1,825 / 3,650 / 5,475 / 7,300) are simply annual snapshots of the same habit.

What happens in the Bear Case preset?

If NVDA returns a negative average annual rate (e.g., -5%), the invested cash flow is slowly eaten by drawdowns. The "Net Advantage of Stock" KPI turns red — the portfolio ends up below the coffee spend. That is the risk-weighted reality behind any 15-year bet on a single stock.

What does "Invest Fraction" do?

Only the invested portion of the $5/day habit is redirected. If you set it to 50%, only $2.50/day ($75/month) is invested while the coffee spend still accrues at full $5/day. This models a hybrid habit — drinking some coffee, investing the rest.

Is the return rate compounded annually or monthly?

Monthly. The slider annual return is converted to a monthly equivalent via ((1 + r)^(1/12)) − 1 and applied end-of-month to a growing balance fed by $150/month. This models realistic DCA behavior rather than a single lump-sum compounding.

How do I export the simulation?

Click "Export CSV Telemetry" to download a per-year time series with coffee spend, contributions, portfolio value, and ratio. Click "Copy Scenario Link" to grab a URL that encodes your current slider state so another analyst sees the exact same scenario.
