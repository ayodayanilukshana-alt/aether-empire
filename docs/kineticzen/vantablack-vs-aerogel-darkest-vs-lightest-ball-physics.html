❓ Frequently Asked Questions
What makes Vantablack the "darkest" material?
Vantablack is a super-black coating made from vertically aligned carbon nanotubes. It absorbs up to 99.965% of visible light, making it the darkest engineered material on Earth. In this simulation, its high mass (10 kg) represents its dense, solid nature.
Why is Aerogel the "lightest" solid?
Aerogel is a synthetic porous ultralight material derived from a gel, with a density as low as 0.0011 g/cm³ — often called "solid smoke." It is the lightest known solid, composed of 99.8% air. Here, its low mass (0.5 kg) reflects this extreme lightness.
How does the physics engine model these materials?
The simulation uses a Matter.js-inspired rigid-body model with configurable mass, restitution (bounce), and air drag. Vantablack's high mass gives it more momentum and resistance to force, while Aerogel's low mass makes it highly responsive to collisions and gravity.
Can I export the live simulation data?
Yes. Use the "Export CSV Telemetry" button to download a snapshot of current parameters, velocities, kinetic energies, and collision counts. The "Copy Scenario Link" button copies a shareable URL with your current slider settings encoded as query parameters.
What do the preset scenarios represent?
DEF is the baseline (Earth gravity, standard bounce). ZERO-G disables gravity for free-floating dynamics. HEAVY doubles gravity and mass. BOUNCY maximizes restitution for energetic collisions. VANTA optimizes the simulation for the Vantablack sphere's high-mass characteristics.
