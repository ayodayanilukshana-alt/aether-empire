Frequently Asked Questions

What is this simulation actually modeling?

A rigid-body gravity maze. Each matter cube is subjected to two conflicting force fields: an inverse-square attraction toward the Black Hole singularity (left) and a radial outward pressure field emitted by the Quasar core (right). The renderer integrates these per-frame at 60FPS, applies restitution-based collisions, and reports system entropy to quantify how far the field has evolved from order.

How do the Black Hole and Quasar forces differ numerically?

The Black Hole uses an inverse-square law with a softening term to avoid singularity blow-ups; the Quasar uses an inverse-linear pressure falloff with a positive outward sign, meaning it repels matter away from its core. Both are scaled by their respective sliders from 0 to 3 units of gravitational constant. Equilibrium is a matter of matching pull magnitude to push magnitude at the maze centerline.

What does "Matter Entropy" represent?

Entropy is computed from the variance of particle speeds and the spatial spread relative to the maze area. A fresh reset begins near zero (ordered); as the two fields compete, velocities diverge and the metric rises. A rising entropy index suggests the Black Hole's capture events are dominating, since asymmetrical acceleration spreads the distribution of velocities.

How is the CSV telemetry structured?

The export begins with a header block of parameter key-value pairs (gravity, pressure, restitution, matter count), followed by a columnar section of per-particle state rows: id, x, y, vx, vy, kinetic energy and current field affinity. Files can be opened directly in spreadsheets or loaded into any numerical toolchain.

Can I share a specific configuration?

Yes. The "Copy Scenario Link" button serializes the five slider parameters into the URL hash and copies a permalink to your clipboard. Loading a link that contains a hash will automatically restore those exact values and reinitialize the maze.

Why does the preset set differ so much?

Each preset demonstrates a qualitatively different physical regime: Singularity (strong capture), Quasar Surge (strong repulsion), Equilibrium (balanced), Chaos Field (extreme restitution and high matter), and Tidal Disruption (steep gradient). Observing how entropy and field affinity evolve across presets is the core value of the workstation.
