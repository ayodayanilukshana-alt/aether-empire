FAQ
What does this workstation actually simulate?

It models two rigid circular bodies (MARIO and SONIC) launched into a bounded chamber filled with static peg obstacles. Physics are integrated with a stable fixed timestep. Each ball reflects off walls and pegs using its restitution, loses energy to friction, and races toward the escape gate on the right wall. First ball to cross the gate records its escape time.

Why does SONIC have different baseline parameters?

SONIC represents the hypersonic archetype: lower mass (0.82), smaller radius, slightly higher restitution multiplier (1.06) and a tunable speed boost (default 1.28×) applied to launch impulse. MARIO is the balanced archetype at 1.00× across the board. This asymmetry is the core rivalry dynamic you are tuning.

How is the CSV telemetry generated?

Every fixed simulation step appends a row containing timestamp, both ball positions and velocities, collision counters and current parameter values. Pressing Export CSV serializes the recorded frame buffer into a standards-compliant CSV file and triggers a browser download.

Can I share an exact scenario?

Yes — Copy Scenario Link encodes every slider value into the URL query string. Opening that link restores the exact parameter set, then you press Run to reproduce the scenario. The physics integrator is deterministic given identical parameters and step count.

What do the presets represent?

Classic Duel is the baseline balance. Speedway raises impulse and lowers gravity for a fast race. Moon Gravity demonstrates low-g parabolic float. Hyper Bounce maximizes restitution. Sticky Floor maximizes friction and kills momentum. Gauntlet packs obstacles to stress the collision resolver.
