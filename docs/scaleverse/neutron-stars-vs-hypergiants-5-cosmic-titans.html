❓ Frequently Asked Questions

How does a neutron star compare to a hypergiant?
A typical neutron star is about 20 km across — roughly the size of a city. UY Scuti, a hypergiant, is 2.4 billion km in diameter, over 100 million times larger. This simulation uses a logarithmic scale to make both visible.
What is the most massive object in this dataset?
UY Scuti is the largest by diameter (2.4 billion km), followed by Betelgeuse (1.2 billion km). However, mass is different: neutron stars are far denser — a teaspoon would weigh billions of tons.
What does the "Log Scale Zoom" slider do?
It adjusts the logarithmic compression of the visual canvas. Because the size range spans over 100 million times, a linear scale would make small objects invisible. The slider lets you focus on either compact remnants or hypergiants.
Can I export the data for my own analysis?
Yes — click "Export CSV Telemetry" to download a CSV containing all five objects with their diameters, Earth ratios, and Sun ratios. You can also "Copy Scenario Link" to share the exact state.
